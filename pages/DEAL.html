Principles, Structures, and Adaptive Agents:
Scaling AI under Limited Supervision

A unified framework spanning data-efficient priors, graph/relational structure as implicit supervision, and adaptive agentic systems
🎯 Data Efficiency · Graph Supervision · Adaptive Agents ⏳ Half-day (3h30m incl. break)
📖 Tutorial Overview

How can AI systems scale when direct supervision is limited? This half-day tutorial connects data-efficient priors, graph and relational structure, and adaptive agents across settings with scarce labels, weak feedback, and self-generated supervision.

🎓 Target Audience

Researchers, practitioners, and graduate students in machine learning, foundation models, graph learning, multi-agent systems, and AI for science. Basic familiarity with machine learning and neural networks is sufficient.

✨ Distinctive Contribution

A unified path from priors → structure → interaction, with practical guidance for comparing methods, evaluating reliability, and choosing limited-supervision strategies.

📌 Tutorial at a Glance:
A practical framework for building and evaluating AI systems with scarce labels, weak feedback, or self-generated supervision.

📌 Module A Abstract

Principles — Learning with Limited Labels

Learning with limited labels requires models to effectively leverage prior experience and adapt to new tasks from only a small amount of supervision. We begin by formulating few-shot learning as generalization across tasks and examine how prior knowledge enables effective learning from a small support set. We then present four major adaptation mechanisms—optimization-based, metric-based, amortization-based meta-learning, and in-context learning—highlighting what is adapted, how adaptation is performed, and the practical strengths and limitations of each approach. Beyond reviewing representative methods, we provide guidance on choosing an appropriate adaptation mechanism and offer a general recipe for translating real-world applications into few-shot learning problems. Case studies in molecular property prediction and user cold-start recommendation demonstrate how task definition, support–query construction, and application-specific insights jointly shape effective solutions under limited supervision.

🔗 Module B Abstract

Structures — Relational & Graph Implicit Supervision

Graph structure improves agentic learning by increasing the information extracted from scarce labels, rollouts, or interactions. This part of the tutorial organizes methods into three operational lenses. Experience graphs shape data collection by constraining synthesis and exposing intermediate supervision. Decision graphs guide action selection by enabling reusable plans and limited test-time search. Learning graphs propagate feedback by aggregating rollouts and assigning credit from sparse outcomes. We clarify each lens in terms of nodes, edges, and where graph operations act, and discuss representative works.

🤖 Module C Abstract

Adaptive Agents under Limited Feedback: From Flat In-Context Adaptation to Structured Context

Agentic tasks require adaptation throughout interaction, even when model parameters remain fixed and explicit feedback is sparse, delayed, or costly. We first examine how observations redirect decisions within a trajectory and how trajectory-level feedback becomes reusable experience across attempts. We then identify the limitations of flat context—mixed semantic roles, difficult credit assignment, and poor reuse—and introduce Structured Context as typed, relational, and editable external state. Through a select–compose–update loop, structured context organizes states, constraints, workflows, feedback, and lessons into guidance that can be retrieved and revised. Representative methods illustrate three levels of adaptation: decision-level adaptation through interaction, experience reuse across tasks, and system-level adaptation through editable workflows, dynamic multi-agent organization, and adaptive knowledge routing. We conclude with open challenges in structural credit assignment, memory consolidation and forgetting, and cost-aware continual adaptation.

⚖️ Responsible AI & Key Risks

The tutorial explicitly addresses risks arising under limited supervision: bias amplification from scarce labels, error propagation in pseudo-labeling/self-training, spurious correlations in graph data, and instability in self-improving agent loops. We emphasize practical safeguards: confidence calibration, selective human oversight, robust evaluation under weak supervision, and transparent reporting of failure modes and domain limits. These issues are especially critical in scientific, biomedical, and socially consequential settings — limited supervision must never be confused with reduced responsibility. Trustworthy scaling is framed as both a technical and scientific question: improving capability while preserving reliability, transparency, and appropriate human control.
