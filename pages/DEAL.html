Scalable Intelligence under Limited Supervision

Principles, Structures, and Adaptive Agents. A unified view of how AI systems learn, reason, and improve when labels, feedback, and interaction are scarce, costly, or unreliable.

Model capacity is no longer the only bottleneck. Increasingly, the scarce resource is trustworthy supervision.

Labels may be expensive, feedback delayed, and self-generated experience unstable. This tutorial connects three research traditions that address the same underlying problem: learning from priors, extracting supervision from structure, and adapting through interaction.

  • 1 Build a conceptual map of limited-supervision methods.
  • 2 Compare few-shot, graph-based, and agentic approaches.
  • 3 Select strategies based on feedback, cost, and stability.
  • 4 Recognize evaluation risks and open research questions.

Who should attend

Technical depth, without a narrow entry gate.

Designed for specialists and expert non-specialists who want a coherent view across learning, graphs, foundation models, and agents.

Especially relevant for

  • Machine learning researchers
  • Foundation model practitioners
  • Graph learning researchers
  • Agent and multi-agent researchers
  • PhD students & postdocs
  • AI for science
  • NLP & data mining

Prerequisites

Graduate-level familiarity with machine learning and neural networks is helpful. No prior background in graph learning, instruction tuning, or agentic systems is assumed. Core ideas will be introduced before specialized methods.
